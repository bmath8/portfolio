I'm a developer in New Jersey. I build AI and full-stack applications in Python and TypeScript — and then I operate them, which is the part I've found most people skip.

Right now a fleet of 25 scheduled agents I wrote is running on my own machine. It has been running unattended for months: preparing a brief every morning at 7:00, watching the system, restarting things that die, and drafting work overnight. It runs on a local model, so it costs nothing per run and no data leaves the box.

I learned more from operating that than from building it. Most of what I know about error handling, structured state, and not trusting a process to stay alive came from being woken up by my own bad assumptions.

Tier 1 runs deterministically; tier 2 calls a local model. Every run logs a status and a one-line result, because a job that fails silently isn't a job.

The whole fleet is draft-only. Agents can research, draft and queue, but nothing sends, spends, deploys or deletes without me approving it. That constraint is the most useful design decision in the project.
