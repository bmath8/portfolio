## Phase 1 — Make the brain pop *(his notes 1, 2, 3)* ✅ DONE

**The diagnosis.** The matcap ramp currently tops out around `#dff0fb` but spends most of its
area in `#33566f`–`#12283c`, which sits within ~10% luminance of the `#050a12` page. The mesh
is *the same value as its background*, so it reads as a silhouette rather than an object. This
is a value problem, not a colour problem — which is why he likes the scheme but wants it to
stand out.

**Changes, in order of impact:**

1. **Widen the matcap value range.** Push the highlight to near-white and deepen the terminator
   so the ramp spans real light-to-dark instead of hovering in the mid-blues. This alone does
   most of the work.
2. **Add a fresnel rim term** in the existing `onBeforeCompile` — edges catch **cyan**
   (`#22e6c8`), so the silhouette separates hard from the background and the accent colour he
   likes appears *on the object* rather than only in the UI. This is the single most effective
   "pop" device and costs nothing.
3. **Two-tone the shading** — cool **violet** (`#8b7bff`) in the deepest sulci against warm-
   bright cyan on the ridges. Answers *"lighter and brighter parts and accents"* directly and
   uses colours already in the approved palette.
4. **Radial contrast behind the mesh** — darken the void immediately behind it and lift the
   surrounding field very slightly, so the brain sits *in front of* the page rather than in it.
5. **Move it further right and up its presence** — camera x offset from `-1.16` to about
   `-1.55`, scale up ~12%, and re-balance the readability scrim so the copy still wins.

**Acceptance:** screenshot side-by-side with the current version. The brain must be
unmistakably an object with a lit side and a dark side, and the cyan rim must be visible at a
glance. Headline contrast must not regress — verify text over the mesh at 1440px and 360px.

## Phase 2 — Fix the nodes so the concept actually reads *(the known bug)* ✅ DONE

The whole idea is *one node per real agent*, and right now they are invisible — buried in
sulci and occluded by the mesh. This is the highest-value fix on the list because it is the
difference between "a brain" and "his system".

1. **Lift nodes off the surface** along the vertex normal instead of scaling the position
   vector, so they sit *on* the cortex rather than inside a fold.
2. **Distribute them deliberately.** The current name-hash lands several in the same region.
   Use farthest-point sampling over the surface so all 26 are legible and spatially separated.
3. **Render them as bright unlit markers with a soft halo sprite**, depth-tested against the
   mesh so back-facing nodes correctly disappear — that occlusion is what proves the brain is
   solid.
4. **Colour by agent class** using the approved accents: cyan = scheduled daily, violet =
   hourly/4-hourly, amber = weekly, magenta = monthly.

**Acceptance:** count the visible nodes in a screenshot. At least 12–15 of 26 legible from the
default camera, and every one clearly *on* the surface.

## Phase 3 — The 3D upgrade *(his note 5 — "another level")* ✅ DONE — `v10-phase3.html`
> Tracts ordered by real time-of-day, one instanced draw call, Gaussian travelling pulse on
> `vLineDistance`, hover-to-name with the real cron line, weighted cursor parallax, scroll
> deliberately not wired to the 3D. **Still open:** GSAP master timeline and the staged
> cron-order intro — specced, not built, not required.

**The concept, in one sentence: the hero shows a day passing through his machine.**

This is the answer to *"show a brain and how it works"* and it is the thing no other portfolio
can copy, because it needs his actual crontab.

1. **Tracts.** Build a `LineSegments2` bundle connecting related agent nodes along paths that
   follow the cortical surface. **One instanced draw call regardless of count** —
   `LineSegmentsGeometry extends InstancedBufferGeometry`, already vendored.
2. **Travelling signals.** Patch the existing dash discard with a Gaussian pulse on
   `vLineDistance` (technique verified and written up in `BRAIN-TECHNIQUE-2026-08-05.md`). A
   signal *travels*; the resting tract stays dim and keeps describing the anatomy.
3. **Wire it to the real schedule.** A slow 24-hour cycle: each node fires when its cron line
   says it does, and signals propagate outward from it. **The 06:40–07:00 cluster becomes a
   visible storm because twelve agents genuinely wake in those twenty minutes.** The motion
   carries meaning — which is the standing objection to *"animations for the sake of."*
4. **Interaction — hover.** Raycast a node → it names the agent, its real cron expression, and
   its last run. This turns a pretty object into something a recruiter can *interrogate*.
5. **Interaction — cursor.** Gentle parallax; the brain turns slightly toward the pointer.
   Weight and inertia, not float-and-bob (the `oryzo.ai` lesson).
6. **Orchestrate with GSAP**, already vendored and never used. One master timeline owning the
   uniforms rather than ad-hoc lerps in the RAF loop — that orchestration *is* the missing
   craft layer.
7. **Intro:** nodes light in cron order on load, so the first three seconds explain the piece.

**Explicitly NOT doing:** scroll will not drive the 3D. He rejected that once already
(*"the animations as you scroll get worse"*), and the research says the same. Scroll moves the
page; the brain has its own clock.

**Acceptance:** it must still hit the budget — ≤700KB 3D payload, ≤25 draw calls, DPR capped
at 1.5, no bloom, one full-screen pass maximum, loop paused when hidden, static poster with JS
off, reduced-motion honoured. **Ambitious AND working, never ambitious instead of working.**

## Phase 4 — Flow between sections *(his note 4)*

Sections currently butt together at hard 1px borders, which is why it reads as stacked blocks
rather than one page.

1. **Delete the hard section borders.** Replace with long gradient seams so each band dissolves
   into the next.
2. **Let the hero bleed.** The dark void under the brain should carry down into the top of
   Selected Work instead of stopping at a line.
3. **Carry the signal motif down the page** — a thin cyan thread linking hero → work →
   capabilities → contact, so the eye is handed from section to section.
4. **Scroll continuity via Lenis** (vendored, unused) for smooth momentum, plus staged reveals
   that are *offset*, not the blanket fade-in-on-scroll that's a documented slop tell — and
   with a no-JS fallback, since a previous version shipped a blank page that way.

**Acceptance:** scroll the full page and screenshot the three seams. No visible hard line
anywhere; each transition reads as continuous.

## Phase 5 — Rebuild the three demos *(his note 6)* ✅ DONE

Currently they are thin canvas sketches. Each becomes a real exhibit.

**1 · Brian OS — the day, interactive.** Promote the 24h dial into the page's second-strongest
object: hover any tick to name the agent and show its cron line and last run; the current-time
hand sweeps live; the 06:40 cluster is annotated as the story it tells. Same data as the hero,

**2 · Squares — the real app.** This is the strongest asset on the site and it is currently a
fake grid of coloured cells. Replace with a **click-to-load `<iframe>` of the live app** behind
a poster frame, so employers interact with the actual thing that ran on game day while the
page still makes zero third-party requests until asked. This is explicitly endorsed:
*"live demos so employers can visualize it not just words."*

**3 · BoomBox — a diagram that actually explains.** The durable/transient split is the
interesting decision and the current three-box sketch doesn't land it. Rebuild as a properly
laid out architecture diagram with labelled edges, packets flowing on the transient path and
persisting on the durable one, and a caption naming the tradeoff.

**Across all three:** consistent framing, real captions, a stated "what you're looking at"
line, and each keyed to its project's accent colour.

**Acceptance:** each demo screenshotted alone and judged on its own. If it wouldn't stand up
as a single image in a case study, it isn't done.

## Order of work, and why

**Phase 1 → 2 first.** They are fast, they are the notes he raised first, and they change how
everything else is judged. No point animating something that doesn't read yet.

**Then 5 (demos)** — more total surface area than the hero and currently the weakest part of
the page.

**Then 4 (flow)** — it is a finishing pass and only makes sense once the sections are final.

**Phase 3 (the 3D upgrade) is the big one and goes last** among the build steps, because it is
the highest-risk and it benefits from everything above being settled. It is also the only
phase where I would stop and show intermediate screenshots before going further.

**One variable per file. Screenshot every time. Never edit the approved base in place.**
