*August 2026 · full top-to-bottom review*

## What's working today

The content strategy is genuinely strong and worth keeping in any redesign. The "every number is checkable" honesty angle is rare and memorable. The narrative — build it, test it, operate it — is exactly what hiring managers say they want in 2026: proof of real, running work over framework lists. The copy is tight and confident. Don't lose any of that.

## What's broken (the reasons it "never felt right")

**1. The scroll-jacked hero holds visitors hostage.** The pinned 3D brain consumes several full screens of scrolling before any work appears. Recruiters spend seconds, not minutes — anything that delays "Selected work" costs interviews.

**2. Huge dead zones.** Between sections there are multiple full viewports of pure black. Jumping with PageDown/PageUp lands on empty screens, and reveal-on-scroll content stays invisible if the intersection observers don't fire. During review I also hit a stray duplicated header and a white bar artifact mid-page.

**3. The brain fights the message.** It's technically impressive, but at hero size it competes with the headline for contrast and attention, and the payoff (hover a node to name an agent) is buried. The concept is right — the *execution scale* is wrong. It works better as a contained, legible visualization than as a page-dominating 3D scene.

**4. One idea carries too much weight.** The page leans on the brain for personality, so everything after the hero flattens into plain dark cards. Distinctiveness should come from typography, layout and rhythm — things that survive on every screen, including mobile and low-power devices.

**5. Density and scannability.** The four stat numbers, the strongest asset, appear below the fold behind the animation. They should hit in the first second.

## The four redesign directions

All four keep 100% of your content and the "checkable numbers" ethos. All are single-file, responsive, no scroll-jacking, fast, and work with JS disabled (option 2's canvas degrades gracefully).

**Option 1 — Mission Control** (`option-1-mission-control.html`)
Ops-dashboard aesthetic: status bar, terminal card running your actual commands, a 24-hour cron timeline where every tick is a named agent. The brain is replaced by the thing it represented — the schedule itself. Best fit for the "I keep systems running" story.

**Option 2 — Neural** (`option-2-neural.html`)
Your brain concept, done right: a lightweight 2D neural constellation (26 hoverable named nodes, a traveling pulse) contained beside the headline instead of behind it. Aurora gradients, Syne display type. Keeps the poetry, kills the scroll-jack and the 3D weight.

**Option 3 — Operator's Dossier** (`option-3-editorial.html`)
Light editorial/Swiss direction: Fraunces serif, cream paper, ledger-style stat table, numbered case studies with "receipts" column. Reads like a printed dossier — maximum recruiter scannability and credibility. The contrarian pick: almost no dev portfolios are light-themed, so it stands out.

**Option 4 — Systems That Stay Up** (`option-4-brutalist.html`)
Loud brutalist take: Anton display type, ticker tape of live stats, full-bleed project rows that invert on hover, red/bone/black. The most memorable of the four — personality as the differentiator.

## Recommendation

For a recruiter-first goal: **Option 1 or 3** as the base, borrowing option 2's constellation as a small interactive garnish if you still want the brain DNA. Option 4 if you'd rather be unforgettable than safe.
